Understanding the Self · OBE Final Examination

I am not
finished becoming.

This is a snapshot of who I am at this point in my life: what I believe, what I question, what I am working toward, and what I still do not know. I do not see identity as something already completed. It is something I continue to understand and shape.

“The past is to be accepted, the present is to be appreciated, and the future is to be shaped.”A principle I want to remember
01
Unit 1 · The self I have come to understand

A self shaped by experience,
but not fixed by it.

I understand myself through a mixture of reflection, experience, and the ideas I encounter. My identity is influenced by the world around me, but I do not believe that any one influence has to decide who I become.

Philosophical perspective

My personal philosophy begins with uncertainty. Human understanding is limited, and the conclusions I hold today may need revision tomorrow. I can still make decisions, but I should not confuse my current understanding with absolute truth.

Psychological perspective

Understanding myself also means observing my thoughts, emotions, behavior, and the patterns that emerge through experience. I am not only what I say I believe; I am also shaped by how I respond, what I practice, and what I learn about myself over time.

Society and culture

I do not develop in isolation. Family, community, education, culture, and social expectations influence how I see myself and the world. I can recognize those influences while still deciding which values and priorities I want to carry forward.

Freedom and responsibility

I value individual autonomy: people should have the freedom to decide what matters to them. But freedom does not remove responsibility. Our choices affect others, and we must be willing to recognize the consequences of our actions.

Uncertainty should not prevent us from living. It should remind us that our understanding is never beyond question.My view of knowledge and truth

Meaning is personal

Life may not come with one predetermined meaning. I can still find meaning in curiosity, relationships, ambitions, responsibilities, or a quiet life. I do not think everyone must want the same things.

Accept, learn, adapt

The past cannot be rewritten. I should not expect my former self to have known what I only learned later. I can acknowledge mistakes and their consequences without remaining trapped in regret.

02
Unit 2 · The many dimensions of me

I exist in more than
one dimension.

No single role or description can contain an entire person. My different dimensions overlap, influence one another, and sometimes pull me in different directions.

A

Intellectual self

I value curiosity, questioning assumptions, and revising my understanding when I encounter better information.

B

Emotional self

I am still learning to recognize my reactions and give myself time to understand them before responding.

C

Physical self

My body is part of how I experience life. Its needs and limits deserve attention rather than being ignored.

D

Social self

My relationships, community, and culture shape my perspective, while I remain responsible for how I treat other people.

E

Spiritual / existential self

I think about uncertainty, mortality, hope, and the freedom to find meaning in the time I have.

F

Digital self

The version of me exposed to the internet is still me. Online freedom does not mean absolute freedom; I must practice restraint and think before posting.

My digital self. Because it is so easy to publish and spread something online, I should not be too quick to post or share. I believe I need to constrain myself even when a platform gives me the freedom to speak. I know I do this all the time—posting or sharing too quickly—and recognizing that habit is part of understanding myself. What I put online can travel further and last longer than the moment in which I posted it.

03
Unit 3 · The self I am building

Persistence, direction,
and room to change.

I do not have to know the final version of myself. I can work toward something, learn from the process, and revise my direction when I understand more.

A strength

Persistence

I can keep working toward a difficult goal instead of giving up as soon as progress becomes slow. Persistence helps me continue learning and improving, especially when a problem takes time to understand.

A trait to manage

Stubbornness

My stubbornness can help me stay committed, but it can also make it difficult to change direction. My philosophy reminds me that persistence should not mean refusing new evidence. I want to know when to hold my ground and when to revise my position.

A current goal

Online programming work

I want to develop my programming skills into practical work opportunities online. This goal gives me a direction to work toward, while remaining open to learning new tools, improving my abilities, and adapting to the opportunities I encounter.

An aspiration

Independent, but teachable

I want to become someone who can think independently without assuming that I am always right; someone persistent enough to pursue goals, but flexible enough to learn from mistakes and other people.

I can be determined without being incapable of changing my mind.A balance I want to practice
04
Unit 4 · The self I need to take care of

Sometimes, the best first step
is to slow down.

My philosophy already gives me a practical response to stress and uncertainty: do not rush into judgment. Observe first, understand what is happening, and then decide what to do with the information available.

01

Slow down

Give myself a moment instead of reacting immediately. Not every problem needs an instant answer.

02

Observe

Separate what I know from what I assume. Identify what is within my control and what remains uncertain.

03

Exercise judgment

Use the best information available. I do not need absolute certainty before making a reasonable decision.

04

Accept and adapt

Accept what has happened, take responsibility where needed, and let new knowledge guide my next decision.

My reminder to myself: Acceptance is not passivity. I cannot change the past or control every outcome, but I can still act, ask for support, and shape what happens next. Taking care of myself also means recognizing when I need rest or help instead of expecting myself to solve everything alone.

05
A message to my future self

Dear future me,

Written in October 2026. To be read again in October 2031.

A letter across five years

I hope you kept becoming.

I do not know what your life looks like now, or which of the things I currently believe you have kept. I hope you have allowed yourself to change without feeling that you must defend every version of who you used to be.

Remember that you were never required to have absolute certainty. You only needed to make the best decisions you could with the knowledge available at the time. If those decisions led somewhere unexpected, accept what happened, take responsibility where necessary, and learn from it.

Do not let the pursuit of a future make you forget the life you are already living. Your time is limited. Appreciate the ordinary moments, the people you care about, the things that make you curious, and the freedom to decide what matters to you.

If your ambitions have changed, examine why. If you have made mistakes, do not confuse accountability with endless self-punishment. If life has become difficult, remember that accepting reality does not mean surrendering hope.

I cannot tell you who to become. That decision belongs to you. I only hope you remain willing to question yourself, revise your understanding, and make room for a better answer.

Whatever has happened, remember this: the past is to be accepted, the present is to be appreciated, and the future is to be shaped.

— Me, in 2026

A snapshot, not a final answer

I am not finished becoming.

And perhaps I do not need to be.
